# Rewriting your roadmap after studying a direct competitor

I spent an evening walking through bbradar.io — the closest direct competitor to BountyIndex. Not to copy them. To decide, feature by feature, whether I was going to compete, cede, or outflank each thing they did.

Then I rewrote my roadmap.

## The three positions

For every feature bbradar shipped, I picked one of:

- **Compete.** They have it, I need it, we do it differently and better. (Program discovery, filtering, per-program pages.)
- **Cede.** They own this and I can't out-invest them. Don't try. (Enterprise sales, paid tier features that require a support team.)
- **Outflank.** They have a version, I ship a wider or freer version. Or they gate it, I make it free. (Per-program RSS. Coverage of platforms they miss.)

The word "outflank" is doing real work here. It's not "do the same thing better." It's "reframe the axis of comparison so their advantage isn't relevant." bbradar's RSS is a Pro feature. Mine is free and public. The comparison is no longer "who has better RSS" — it's "why would I pay for RSS?" That's a different conversation, and it's the conversation I want to have.

## What made the roadmap

**Now:** Free versions of their gated features. Coverage of platforms they don't support (Immunefi was the obvious one). UX polish they haven't prioritized.

**Next:** The moat features — community response-time tracking, per-program notes, saved filter sets. Things that get more valuable the longer a user uses the site.

**Later:** MCP server, browser extension, Burp plugin. Distribution channels rather than product features.

**Never:** Email alerts (see the RSS post for why). Paid tier. Enterprise features. Chat with the community they can eventually build.

## The move that mattered

Writing "Never" down as a real section on the roadmap. It's the least glamorous but most useful discipline of competitive analysis: **explicitly reject the features you don't want to build.** Every "Never" is a permanent no to a category of scope creep and a category of user request. It also tells users what kind of product this is going to be. Some users self-select out based on the Never list; that's a feature.

The full bbradar analysis lives in a memory doc I don't publish. The roadmap it produced is the public artefact. Do the analysis privately. Publish the decisions.
